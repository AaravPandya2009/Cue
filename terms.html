A tool for reviewing warning signs.

Cue provides an experimental assessment of recorded or written conversations. Its scores and scheme labels are not proof of fraud or a guarantee that a caller is safe.

01 / What Cue provides

You may use this prototype for personal evaluation and demonstrations. It supports uploaded recordings, transcript analysis, possible scheme labels, basic audio-quality checks, presentation playback, and suggested actions.

It does not monitor live phone calls, authenticate callers, look up phone-number reputation, block calls, contact authorities, or recover funds. The live demo replays a previously analyzed recording; it is a simulation.

02 / Use recordings responsibly

Do not use the prototype for unlawful surveillance, harassment, impersonation, or other unlawful activity. Do not present its output as verified evidence that a person or organization has committed a crime.

03 / Understand the assessment

Transcription can mishear speech or generate incorrect text. The detector uses a limited set of English-language patterns and can miss scams or flag ordinary conversation. Quoted statements, jokes, negation, and incomplete context can affect the result. Review and correct the transcript and speaker labels.

Risk scores are heuristic indicators, not measured probabilities. A low score does not establish safety. “Possible pyramid scheme” and other labels describe matched language patterns, not legal findings. Pace and audio quality do not establish a speaker's honesty or reliability.
